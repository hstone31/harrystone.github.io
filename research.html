Mapping plant growth with repeat drone LiDAR

Snodgrass watershed, Colorado · 2025–present

We flew drone-based LiDAR repeatedly over an 81-ha mountain watershed through the 2025 growing season. Differencing canopy height models gives a growth proxy that we compare against soil moisture, groundwater, streamflow, and ET records — through time, and across land-cover types, distance from the stream, and terrain-derived micro-climate indices (topographic wetness, slope, aspect).

The work builds on earlier observations that ET stayed high at several flux towers while groundwater fell during a long dry spell, suggesting some plants tap groundwater through summer.
